## 1. The Problem: Paper Receipts and Manual Data Entry

Anyone running a sole proprietorship or small business knows this cycle. Over the course of a month, a handful of receipts pile up: fuel, office supplies, small tools, coffee at a client meeting. Individually they look inconsequential, but together they're real costs that lower your tax base.

The trouble is that the road from a receipt to a logged expense is long and full of places where things get lost:

- **receipts fade and get lost** — thermal print often becomes illegible after a few weeks,
- **manual data entry is tedious** — you have to type the seller, NIP, date, net amount, VAT, and gross amount into the system,
- **putting it off "until the end of the month"** turns into a pile nobody wants to type up,
- **a lost receipt is a lost cost** — meaning a genuinely higher tax bill.

The result is always similar: some costs never make it into your bookkeeping, and the ones that do cost a lot of dull, repetitive work. This is exactly the kind of task worth handing over to artificial intelligence — we cover this in more detail in the article [How AI Helps Small Business Accounting](/blog/jak-ai-pomaga-w-ksiegowosci-malej-firmy).

## 2. How Receipt OCR Works

**OCR** (*optical character recognition*) is a technology that "reads" text from a photo. In eKsięgowy AI, OCR is combined with the AI assistant, so the bot doesn't just see letters and numbers — it also **understands** what's the seller, what's the NIP number, and what's the VAT amount.

The flow is simple and comes down to three steps on your side: **photo → verification → confirmation**. Behind the scenes, here's what happens:

1. **The photo reaches the bot** on Telegram as an ordinary picture.
2. **OCR + AI recognize the key fields:** the seller's name, their **NIP**, the purchase **date**, the **amount** (net, VAT, gross), and the **VAT rates** applied.
3. **The assistant proposes an expense** — it maps the recognized data onto the fields of a cost document and shows them to you for approval.
4. **After confirmation, the expense goes into wFirma** through the integration, exactly as if you'd entered it manually in the panel.

The key point is that the document isn't created "silently." You always see the recognized data **before** anything gets posted — so you stay in control of what lands in your records.

### Step 2. Photograph the Receipt and Send It to the Bot

Right after the purchase, take a photo of the receipt and send it to the bot like any regular photo in the chat. A few tips for the best recognition:

- **good lighting** and the receipt laid flat (no folds or shadows),
- **the whole receipt in frame** — including the header (the seller's name and NIP) and the VAT summary at the bottom,
- **a sharp photo** — wait for your phone to lock focus, especially with small print.

### Step 3. Check the Recognized Data

The bot will reply with a summary of what it read: seller, NIP, date, net amount, VAT, gross amount, and rates. **This is the most important moment in the whole process.** Take a look to make sure everything matches — especially the gross amount and the NIP. If something looks off (e.g., a blurred digit), correct the data or take a better photo.

### Step 4. Confirm the Entry

Once the data checks out, you confirm — and the bot creates the expense in wFirma. You get a message back confirming the document was saved. The receipt is now logged, and you can safely throw away the paper slip or keep it in line with document retention rules.

## 4. What the Bot Recognizes, and When to Check Manually

OCR handles typical, legible receipts very well. By default, the assistant picks up:

- the **seller's name** and their **NIP**,
- the transaction **date**,
- the **amounts**: net, VAT, and gross,
- the **VAT rates** applied on the receipt.

There are, however, situations where it's worth taking a closer look or correcting the data manually:

- **faded or crumpled receipts** — thermal print can become illegible, and OCR may misread digits,
- **receipts without the buyer's NIP** — keep in mind that a plain receipt isn't always a sufficient cost document; in many cases you need an **invoice** (a receipt with NIP up to a certain limit is sometimes treated as a simplified invoice). Verify the requirements for your form of bookkeeping.
- **multiple VAT rates or exempt line items** — check whether the breakdown of amounts was read correctly,
- **foreign currencies and purchases abroad** — exchange rates and how they're recorded need separate attention.

The rule is simple: **AI speeds up data entry, but you approve the document.** The verification step exists precisely to catch exceptions before they land in your records.
