## 1. Answers questions about VAT, PIT, CIT, and ZUS in plain language

The most common obstacle for a small business isn't a lack of knowledge — it's **not having time to look it up**. "Can I deduct VAT on this client lunch?", "What's the difference between the flat-rate tax (ryczałt) and general rules?", "What is the reduced ZUS (mały ZUS) scheme?" — there's an answer to each of these questions, but it's usually scattered across forums, guides, and PDFs from the ministry.

An AI assistant answers such questions **immediately and in plain terms** — no jargon, with specifics tailored to a sole proprietorship or a small company. Ask "how much is the health insurance contribution under the flat-rate scheme?" and you'll get an explanation instead of a link to a 40-page ministry brochure.

Important: treat this as a **first orientation**, not a binding interpretation. AI is great at explaining the rules and suggesting what to ask your accountant — but any specific amount or deadline is still worth confirming with an advisor, since rates and thresholds change every year.

## 2. Issues and sends invoices (wFirma, KSeF e-invoices)

Issuing invoices is a classic example of "small tasks" that add up to hours. An assistant integrated with **wFirma** (a Polish accounting platform) issues an invoice on your command, right in the conversation: you provide the counterparty, line items, and amounts, and the system handles the rest — numbering, VAT rates, seller details.

Then there's **KSeF (Krajowy System e-Faktur, the National e-Invoice System)**. B2B invoices increasingly need to be submitted to this system in a structured format, and the assistant can **send the e-invoice to KSeF** directly from the integration — no manual XML export or logging into yet another portal. It's worth tracking KSeF deadlines and the scope of the obligation separately, since the rollout schedule has changed over time.

Result: "issue an invoice for company X with 2 line items" instead of ten clicks across several tabs.

## 3. Verifies counterparties against the VAT whitelist

Before you order a larger transfer, it's worth checking whether the counterparty's account appears on the **VAT taxpayer whitelist (Biała lista)** — because paying into the "wrong" account number on a larger transaction risks losing the tax-deductible cost and joint liability for VAT. Manually pasting NIPs into the government search tool before every payment is tedious, and it's easy to skip it "just this once."

The assistant checks the counterparty against the whitelist **directly in the conversation**: it reports the taxpayer status and confirms the account before you order the transfer. That's verification in a few seconds instead of a separate trip to the Ministry of Finance website.

We cover the rules, the limit, and the penalties in a separate guide: [VAT whitelist — how to check a counterparty](/blog/biala-lista-vat-jak-sprawdzic-kontrahenta).

## 4. Auto-fills company data by NIP from the GUS registry

A new client, a new record — and once again you're copying the name, address, and REGON (business registry number) from some email or business card. That's a quick route to a typo in the company name on an invoice.

The assistant **pulls company data using just the NIP** from the GUS (Central Statistical Office) registry and fills in the counterparty record for you: full name, address, legal form. Instead of retyping, you simply confirm. The record is complete and consistent from day one, and invoices are free of "typed from memory" errors.

## 5. Logs receipts from a photo in Telegram (OCR)

Receipts are the bane of small businesses: they get lost, fade, and end up loose in the car's glovebox. And each one is a potential cost that lowers your tax bill — provided it actually makes it into the books.

This is where **Telegram OCR** comes in. You take a photo of the receipt with your phone, send it to the assistant in chat, and it **reads the data** — the vendor, amount, VAT, date — and creates an expense from it. No retyping, no waiting until evening: you can log the cost while standing in the checkout line or right after filling up the tank.

This is one of those tasks AI arguably handles better than a human at 22:00 — it doesn't get tired and doesn't mix up digits. Full details of the flow: [Receipt OCR in Telegram — log an expense from a photo](/blog/ocr-paragonow-telegram-zaksieguj-wydatek).

## 6. Tracks tax deadlines (calendar and reminders)

VAT, JPK (Standard Audit File for Tax), PIT advance payments, ZUS contributions — for a small business, it's a monthly obstacle course where it's easy to miss a date. And being late means interest and stress that could have been avoided.

The assistant knows the **Polish tax deadline calendar** and reminds you of upcoming dates before it's too late. In the Telegram bot version, it can **message you in advance** — "ZUS contribution payment is due in 3 days" — instead of relying on you to check the calendar yourself. The exact deadline dates depend on the year and whether they fall on a weekend or holiday, so the system accounts for such shifts.

This turns deadlines from "things to remember" into "things that remind themselves."

## 7. Produces quick financial summaries and reports

The question "how am I actually doing this month?" usually ends with not wanting to look for the answer. Pulling out simple numbers — revenue, costs, unpaid invoices — can be disproportionately tedious.

You can ask the assistant directly: "how many invoices did I issue this month?", "which clients still haven't paid me?", "what's my revenue this quarter?". You'll get a **clear summary** instead of a spreadsheet export and manual totaling. That gives you something more valuable than the report itself — an **ongoing sense of control** over your company's finances, without digging through tables.

## What AI Won't Replace

Let's be honest: an AI assistant is a **tool for executional, repetitive work**, not a replacement for an accountant or tax advisor. Some things stay on the human side:

- **Choosing a tax regime and optimization** — the decision between the flat-rate scheme (ryczałt), a flat rate (liniowy), or general rules depends on your individual situation. AI will explain the differences; a binding recommendation comes from an advisor.
- **Interpretations in ambiguous cases** — unusual costs, foreign transactions, disputes with the tax office. These require expert knowledge and professional accountability.
- **Signing the tax return and representation** — annual filings, audits, and contact with the tax office are the accountant's or advisor's role.
- **Final verification** — AI can make mistakes. Treat its answers as first-line help, and confirm specific amounts and deadlines with a specialist.

The best setup is **AI + accountant**: the assistant handles the daily, small-scale work (invoices, receipts, reminders), and the accountant deals with what genuinely requires a human. Less work for both sides, fewer mistakes, and cheaper service, since the accountant doesn't waste time retyping receipts.
